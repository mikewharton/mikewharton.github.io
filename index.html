Current research

My DPhil explores capability-aware assistance: understanding what people and AI systems can do, and using this knowledge to design safer, more effective assistance that preserves human skills and control. I'm particularly interested in high-stakes settings such as healthcare and critical infrastructure.

  1. Can we infer people's abilities, confidence, and stress from behavioural and physiological signals?
  2. How does AI assistance affect people's skills over time?
  3. How can we measure AI models' capabilities and uncertainty?
  4. How can we make AI systems easier to understand, monitor, and oversee?

Supervised by Digby Chappell (HEAT, Oxford Robotics Institute), Chris Summerfield (HIP), and Maike Osborne (BGL). Affiliated with Christian Schroeder de Witt (Witt Lab).

2026 Measuring downstream effects of AI-mediated communicationWharton, M.M. et al. · AI4GOOD Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026 (poster)

AI-generated summaries are increasingly common, for example when coding agents compress their context. I developed a scalable method for testing model confidence on problems that cannot be solved from prior knowledge. Across nine models and three synthetic domains, both performance and confidence declined sigmoidally as summaries became shorter, but confidence remained high even when performance approached its minimum.

2024 Perception-centric design considerations for low-cost haptic emulation in prototypesWharton, M.M. et al. · Proceedings of the Design Society 4, 845–854

At Bristol's Design and Manufacturing Futures Lab, I developed a modular system for integrating low-cost Arduino haptics into design prototypes. Researching human touch perception sparked my interest in human-computer interaction. The work was presented at DESIGN 2024 in Croatia.

2024 Photogrammetry and mean intensity mapping as methods of low-cost structural health monitoringWharton, M.M. · Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing 219, 111583

Developed a low-cost method for measuring vibration from ordinary video by averaging the brightness of a region across frames. Using a camera with high optical zoom, I tested its potential for structural health monitoring on the Clifton Suspension Bridge.

Paper
2024 Validation of chain drive resonance models by video, audio, and torque measurementsWragge-Morley, R.T. et al. · 21st International Conference on Experimental Mechanics

Working with a team of engineers in collaboration with GB Cycling, I investigated bicycle-chain resonance using slow-motion video, acoustic measurements, and torque analysis. We compared the results with a static physical model, a discrete finite element model, and a mid-century analytical model, which proved remarkably accurate despite its low computational cost.
